My group studies spatially extended nonlinear systems, where simple local rules produce spots, stripes, traveling waves, self-replicating structures, and spatiotemporal chaos. We ask how small, targeted interventions can steer these systems toward new behavior, and whether we can predict the outcome in advance.

## Localized control as a design tool

Control of chaos is usually applied to an entire system, driving it to a steady state or a simple oscillation. We apply control only inside a small region and ask what the rest of the system does. In the Gray–Scott model, localized time-delayed feedback creates stable periodic structures inside an otherwise chaotic state ([*Phys. Rev. E* 104, 064213, 2021](/publications/)). We are extending this to localized parameter changes, electric-field-induced patterning, the Selkov glycolysis model, two-dimensional systems, and the complex Ginzburg–Landau equation.


## Applications

The same framework applies wherever localized intervention matters in practice: targeted interventions in spatial epidemic models, vegetation patterns in ecology, and chemical reaction–diffusion experiments.
